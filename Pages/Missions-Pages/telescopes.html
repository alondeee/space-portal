Euclid Telescope

A telescope built by ESA and NASA to detect Dark Matter The target of the Euclid Telescope is understand dark energy and dark matter by measuring the expansion of the universe.
The mission is named after the ancient Greek mathematician Euclid. Euclid was launched on 1 July 2023 from Cape Canaveral in Florida using SpaceX's Falcon 9. The first images from Euclid was received and release to the public 7 November 2023.
It current orbits the Sun at the Lagrange Point 2 (L2). Euclid's detection wavelength is at Visible or Near-Infrared, it carries the VIS (Visible imager) and NISP (Near-Infrared Spectrometer) instruments.
It's goal is to map 1.5 billion Galaxies to study Dark matter and over a peroid of 6 years (Mission Duration)

James Webb Space Telescope (JWST)


JWST was launched on 25 December 2021 on an Ariane 5 rocket from Kourou, French Guiana.
(NASA) led JWST design and development and partnered with two central agencies: the European Space Agency (ESA) and the Canadian Space Agency (CSA).
The James Webb Space Telescope has four key goals,

  • mainly to search for light from the first stars and galaxies that formed in the universe after the Big Bang,
  • to study galaxy formation and evolution,
  • to understand star formation and planet formation,
  • to study planetary systems and the origins of life

    Hubble Space Telescope (HST)


    Hubble Space Telescope was launched on April 24, 1990 on Space Shuttle Discovery (STS-31) from John F.Kennedy Space Center, Florida.
    Hubble Space Telescope has made many important discoveries, which resulted in breakthroughs in Astrophysics and our understanding of the universe.

    Such as:

    • Age and expansion of the universe
    • Black holes
    • Extending visible wavelength images
    • Solar System discoveries
    • Supernova reappearance
    • Mass and size of Milky Way

    Gaia Space Telescope

    Gaia is a retired space observatory launched by European Space Agency (ESA). It was launched on 19 December 2013, in Kourou ELS, French Guyana. Gaia was decommissioned on 27 March, 2025 due to Gaia ran out of cold gas that is needed to point and spin in extreme precision.
    Gaia has detected 1,811,709,771 stellar positions, which is expected to detect 500,000 quasars, 10,000 Jupiter like planets, new asteroids and comets. This is a major breakthrough in astronomy and astrophysics, as it has provided a 3D map of the Milky Way Galaxy with unprecedented accuracy. It will help astronomers understand the Milky Way better, and its formation and evolution since the Big Bang.
    The data archive of Gaia has exceeded 1 Petabyte (PB) of data, which the total cost is 1-1.4 Billion USD (€740 to €950 million Euros).
