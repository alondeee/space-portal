Vera C.Rubin Observatory

Vera C. Rubin Observatory is an observatory that is located in Coquimbo Region, Chile. Its main mission is to conduct a ten year survey of the Southern Sky.
The scientific goals of the Vera C. Rubin Observatory are:

  • Study dark energy and dark matter by measuring weak gravitational lensing, baryon acoustic oscillations, and photometry of type Ia supernovae.
  • Map small objects in the Solar System, particularly near-Earth asteroids and Kuiper belt objects.
  • Detect transient astronomical events including novae, supernovae, gamma-ray bursts, quasar variability, and gravitational lensing.
  • Map the Milky Way.

Very Large Telescope

The Very Large Telescope (VLT) is an optical and infrared observatory which consist of four individual telescopes, each equipped with a primary mirror that measures 8.2 metres (27 ft) in diameter.
each individual telescope has a specific name, which are Antu, Kueyen, Melipal, and Yepun. Each Name is in Mapuche Language, which is the language of the Native People in Northern Chile. The names of the telescopes are as follows: the Sun, the Moon, the Southern Cross, and Venus It is located in Cerro Paranal in the Atacama Desert in Northern Chile.

ESO

Foundational Data

  • Agency:European Southern Observatory (ESO)
  • First Light:May 1998
  • Mission Type:An Optical and Infrared Observatory
  • Primary Mirror Diameter:8.20 m
  • Instruments:
    • SPHERE: Direct exoplanet imager using extreme adaptive optics.
    • MUSE: 3D integral field spectrograph for studying distant galaxies
    • X-shooter: Single-target spectrograph covering ultraviolet to near-infrared at once.
    • ESPRESSO: Ultra-stable high-resolution spectrograph searching for rocky exoplanets
    • GRAVITY: Combines light from multiple telescopes for ultra-high spatial resolution.
    • MATISSE: Mid-infrared interferometric receiver combining four telescope beams.
    • 4LGSF: Laser guide star facility creating artificial stars to correct for atmospheric blur
  • Wavelengths:300 nm – 20 μm (N-UV, visible light, NIR, SWIR, MWIR, and LWIR)
  • Field of View:27 arcminutes
  • Goal:Investigating the center of our galaxy, capturing direct images of extrasolar planets, and probing the early universe.

For more Information, visit VLT Main Page or Wikipedia

Mauna Kea Observatories

Mauna Kea Observatories are a group of independent large telescope observatories located on the summit of Mauna Kea in Hawaii. There are a total of 13 telescopes at the Mauna Kea Observatories, which are funded and run by various organizations and governments from other nations. These are the 13 telescopes:

  • Keck Observatory
  • Subaru Telescope
  • Gemini North Observatory
  • Canada-France-Hawaii Telescope
  • University of Hawaii 2.2m Telescope
  • NASA Infrared Telescope Facility
  • James Clerk Maxwell Telescope
  • Caltech Submillimeter Observatory
  • United Kingdom Infrared Telescope (UKIRT)
  • One receiver of the Very Long Baseline Array (VLBA)
  • University of Hawaiʻi 36-inch (910 mm) telescope
  • Sub-Millimeter Array
  • Thirty Meter Telescope (Proposed)

Palomar Observatory

Palomar Observatory is an astronomical observatory located in San Diego County, California, United States. It is owned and operated by the California Institute of Technology (Caltech). The observatory is home to several telescopes, including the famous 200-inch (5.1-meter) Hale Telescope, which was the largest telescope in the world when it was completed in 1948 and the Hale telescope is still in operation today.
The first known quasar (3C 273) was discovered using the Palomar Observatory's 200-inch telescope in 1963 by Maarten Schmidt, and the first brown dwarf quasar (Teide 1) was discovered in 1995 by Rafael Rebolo López, María Rosa Zapatero Osorio, and Eduardo L. Martín using the Palomar Observatory's 200-inch telescope. Palomar Observatory is an iconic facility for scientific advancement, instrument development, and student training.

Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA)

Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array - ALMA Radio Telescope Or known as ALMA Observatory is an international partnership amongst Europe, the United States, Canada, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, and Chile. It is built by European Southern Observatory, National Institutes of Natural Sciences, Japan, National Science Foundation.
ALMA is located in the Atacama Desert of northern Chile. ALMA is one of the 11 telescopes that contributed to the Event Horizon Telescope (EHT), that discovered the first visual evidence of Supermassive Black holes like M87* and Sagittarius A*.
