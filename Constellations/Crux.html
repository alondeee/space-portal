Crux

The Southern Cross - the smallest but one of the most brilliant constellations

Crux

  • Major Stars:
  • 1. α Crucis (Acrux)
  • 2. β Crucis (Becrux/Mimosa)
  • 3. γ Crucis (Gacrux)
  • 4. δ Crucis (Decrux)
  • 5. ε Crucis (Intrometida)
  • 6. ζ Crucis
  • 7. η Crucis
  • 8. θ¹ Crucis
  • 9. θ² Crucis
  • 10. λ Crucis

    • Notable Stars:

  • Acrux (α Cru) — southernmost first-magnitude star, triple system
  • Mimosa (β Cru) — massive star, one of the hottest first-magnitude stars
  • Gacrux (γ Cru) — red giant, nearest first-magnitude star to Earth

    • Famous Deepsky objects:

  • Jewel Box Cluster (NGC 4755) — one of the finest open clusters
  • Coalsack Nebula — dark nebula, most prominent in the sky
  • NGC 4609 — open cluster
  • NGC 4103 — open cluster
  • Dragon's Head Nebula (part of the Coal Sack)

    • Crux — Foundational Data

  • Rank: 88th largest constellation (SMALLEST!)
  • Area: 68 square degrees
  • Brightest Star: Acrux (α Cru) - magnitude 0.77
  • Best Visible: March — June
  • Mythology: Named by European explorers; represents the Christian cross. Visible on flags of Australia, New Zealand, Papua New Guinea, Samoa, and Brazil.
  • The longer bar of the cross points approximately towards the South Celestial Pole.
  • Cannot be seen north of 25°N latitude.
  • Neighbours: Centaurus, Musca
